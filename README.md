# cell_fate
NRF1 triggers primitive endoderm lineage specification by driving mitochondrial metabolism-mediated chromatin remodeling in pre-implantation embryo

Mammalian embryos undergo significant dynamic molecular regulatory changes and lineage specifications accompanied by the reprogramming of epigenetic and metabolic patterns before implantation. The primitive endoderm (PrE) is one of the origins of extraembryonic tissues and critical for embryonic development. However, the mechanism of triggering PrE lineage specification remains incompletely characterized. By leveraging multi-omics profiling, we identified NRF1 driven mitochondrial metabolic genes and PrE-specific factors during the second lineage specification. Activated mitochondrial metabolism generated mitochondrial reactive oxygen species (mtROS), which diffuse into the nucleus and promote chromatin remodeling through the replacement of H3.1/2 with H3.3, facilitating NRF1 binding and PrE specification.
